What I believe

Why I'm excited, and what I'm building toward

Open each one. This is the point of view behind the work above.

Reading and watching only go so far. I'm most excited about AI giving students simulated experiences (a spreadsheet to fix, an app to navigate, an agent to direct) that prepare them for real jobs, safely and repeatedly, before the stakes are real. The Muse Simulator and the Excel simulator are both bets on exactly that. I am just as excited about using AI to help students role play and deal with human situations. It is kind of hard to get two humans to role play really well, but AI is actually really great at role playing. That can improve training for police officers, prepare students for interviews, and give students practice talking through and articulating the thoughts that are in their head, out loud, until the words come out the way they mean them.
Since Meta, I've used AI extensively to write software and automate repetitive, boring work. The gain isn't just speed. It's attention. When grading, drafting, watching an inbox, and routine follow-up are handled, teachers and small operators get their time back for judgment, relationships, and craft.
Working on the human side of AI at Meta taught me to distrust a good demo. What matters is how real people feel the AI handled the task. Good AI work means listening to actual users, knowing where the experience fails them, and keeping a human in charge of what sends, spends, or counts. That's the habit I teach: draft, review, approve, then automate with confidence.
I teach the tools I teach because I believe they genuinely help real people, and I really enjoy making people's lives better. I have believed that since my MyClaw days at Meta, when I watched an early agent take real work off my plate. I'm trying to make the world a better place with this. That's why the textbook is free, why the course is hands-on instead of theoretical, and why I keep building tools for educators. If AI is going to change work, the people who learn it by doing, not just hearing about it, will be ready.
